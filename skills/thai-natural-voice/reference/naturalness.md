## Start from discourse

English often requires an explicit subject and packages logic inside one sentence. Natural Thai can establish a topic, omit what is understood, and distribute the logic across several beats.

Translate the message's job before its words:

| Source-shaped move | Thai-first move |
|---|---|
| `We are pleased to inform you that…` | State the news; add warmth after it if needed. |
| `There are three things you should know.` | `มี 3 เรื่องที่ควรรู้` or name the first thing directly. |
| `This allows users to…` | Name what the user can now do. |
| `In order to…` | Use `เพื่อ…` or state the action and result. |
| `It is important to note that…` | State the important fact. |
| `As a result of…` | Put the cause first, then the consequence. |

Use topic-first openings that fit the channel:

- Time/situation: `รอบนี้`, `ตั้งแต่พรุ่งนี้`, `ช่วงนี้`, `หลังอัปเดตล่าสุด`
- Reader need: `ถ้ากำลังเลือก…`, `ใครเจอปัญหานี้…`
- Verdict: `สรุปสั้น ๆ`, `คำตอบคือ…`, `คุ้ม ถ้า…`
- Event: `เกิดเหตุ…`, `บริษัทประกาศ…`, `ล่าสุด…`

Avoid turning every paragraph into a school essay with an introduction, three symmetrical points, and a repeated conclusion.

## Build natural rhythm

Write one spoken breath per clause. Mix short and medium sentences; reserve long sentences for a relationship that genuinely needs to unfold together.

For social posts:

- Put one beat or one contrast in a line.
- Break before a reveal, consequence, or change of direction.
- Keep related evidence together instead of breaking every sentence.
- Use a fragment when the missing material is obvious: `เหตุผลง่าย ๆ` / `แต่ยังไม่จบ` / `ตรงนี้สำคัญ`.

For articles and work writing:

- Keep paragraphs compact but complete.
- Use headings only when they help scanning.
- Let ordering carry logic; do not announce every transition.

Repetition is natural when it creates rhythm or emphasis. Repetition is robotic when the same frame merely fills space.

## Choose references and particles

Thai pronouns encode relationship. A grammatically valid pronoun can still feel socially wrong.

- Reuse the user's chosen `ผม`, `ฉัน`, `เรา`, `คุณ`, role name, or kin term when clear.
- Omit first- and second-person references when context already supplies them.
- Use `เรา` only when the speaker and reader genuinely share the frame; avoid manufacturing agreement.
- Use `คุณ` where direct address is useful, not as a translation of every English `you`.
- Use role or organization names when accountability matters: news, policy, service incidents.

Particles set stance and relationship:

- `ครับ/ค่ะ` — place at greetings, requests, acknowledgements, sensitive corrections, and closings; not necessarily every sentence.
- `นะ` — soften or invite shared understanding; repeated use can sound coaxing.
- `เลย` — strengthen immediacy or degree; use where the speaker would stress the phrase aloud.
- `ล่ะ/ล่ะก็` — conversational turn or consequence; too casual for neutral reporting.
- `สิ/ซิ`, `จ้า`, `ฮะ`, stylized particles — use only when the persona and audience already license them.

When gender is unknown, a neutral written sentence often sounds more natural than alternating `ครับ/ค่ะ` or writing `ครับ/ค่ะ` after every line.

## Use English and internet language with intent

Thai online writing naturally mixes codes, but the switch should do a job:

- Keep a recognized domain term: `deploy`, `brief`, `insight`.
- Preserve a product or interface label the reader must find.
- Use an English phrase that the target community treats as ordinary shorthand.
- Avoid English added only to make the post look current.

Use emoji as navigation or affect, not confetti. One repeated system can organize a post; unrelated emoji on every bullet creates noise.

Use vowel or consonant elongation (`มากกก`, `ไปปป`) only in playful peer/community channels, normally once. Avoid it in news, professional, safety, health, legal, or financial writing.

Use `555` only when laughter fits the actual relationship. Never add laughter to criticism, bad news, ambiguity, or a power-imbalanced exchange.

## Robot-smell detector

Treat these as frequency alarms, not banned words:

| Smell | What to look for | Better move |
|---|---|---|
| Connector parade | `นอกจากนี้`, `อย่างไรก็ตาม`, `ดังนั้น`, `ด้วยเหตุนี้` opening successive paragraphs | Let order imply the link; keep only the connector that changes interpretation. |
| Abstract padding | `ดำเนินการ`, `มีความสำคัญ`, `มีประสิทธิภาพ`, `ในบริบทของ` | Name who does what and what changes. |
| Demonstrative fog | repeated `สิ่งนี้`, `เรื่องดังกล่าว`, `ดังกล่าวข้างต้น` | Repeat the concrete noun or omit the reference. |
| Pronoun grid | `เรา` or `คุณ` in nearly every sentence | Omit recoverable subjects; vary with topic-first clauses. |
| Particle sprinkler | `ครับ/ค่ะ/นะครับ` at every stop | Keep particles at relational moments. |
| Template contrast | repeated `ไม่ใช่แค่… แต่…`, `ไม่เพียง… แต่ยัง…` | State the stronger claim directly or use one contrast only. |
| Inflated praise | `น่าทึ่ง`, `ทรงพลัง`, `ยกระดับ`, `ตอบโจทย์` without evidence | Replace with the observable benefit. |
| Symmetry addiction | every list has three equal-length items | Use the number of points the content actually has. |
| Recap echo | closing restates the opening with new adjectives | End on the implication, next action, or a clean stop. |
| Translation punctuation | commas joining every clause; colons and dashes carrying English syntax | Rebuild as Thai clauses and paragraph breaks. |
| Instant intimacy | pet names, kin terms, slang, or `ทุกคนนน` without a relationship basis | Use warm-neutral Thai first. |
| Empty CTA | `อย่าลืม…`, `มาร่วม…กัน!` after informational copy | Give a specific useful next action or omit it. |

Common phrase clusters become conspicuous when stacked: `ในยุคที่…`, `ปฏิเสธไม่ได้ว่า…`, `ไม่ว่าจะเป็น…`, `สะท้อนให้เห็นว่า…`, `ก้าวไปอีกขั้น`, `อย่างแท้จริง`. One may fit; several in one piece usually flatten the voice.

## Repair sequence

Repair in this order:

1. Write the paragraph's job in five Thai words or fewer.
2. Delete the ceremonial lead-in and repeated conclusion.
3. Put the actor, event, verdict, or reader need first.
4. Replace abstract nouns with one concrete verb.
5. Remove recoverable pronouns.
6. Reconnect clauses as spoken beats.
7. Add back only the particle, connector, or emphasis that the relationship needs.

If steps 2–7 still produce translation-shaped Thai, discard the sentence and redraft from step 1.
